REFERENCE GUIDE // POLLEN, MOLD & AIR QUALITY DEFINITIONS

What Each Allergen & Air Quality Metric Means

Informational reference only — not medical or veterinary advice
POLLEN Late Spring – Summer

🌱 Grass / Turf Pollen (Poaceae)

Pollen released by lawn and pasture grasses (Bermuda, Timothy, Kentucky Bluegrass, Rye). Concentrations are highest on warm, dry, breezy mornings and during lawn mowing.

Human Impact: Common trigger for summer hay fever (sneezing, nasal congestion, itchy eyes).
🐾 Dog Care Tip: Dogs walking through tall grass can pick up pollen on their paws and coat; wiping paws with a damp cloth after walks helps reduce irritation.
POLLEN Feb – May

🌳 Tree Pollen (Oak, Birch, Alder, Pine)

Lightweight, wind-borne pollen released in spring by deciduous and coniferous trees. Travels easily on breezy days and settles as a fine dusting on outdoor surfaces.

Human Impact: Major cause of early-spring seasonal allergies, sinus congestion, and watery eyes.
🐾 Dog Care Tip: Brushing your dog's coat after spring walks helps keep outdoor pollen off indoor furniture and bedding.
POLLEN Aug – First Frost

🌾 Ragweed & Weeds (Ambrosia, Mugwort)

Fine, wind-dispersed pollen produced by late-summer and autumn weeds. Remains a leading seasonal allergen across North America until temperatures drop below freezing (`≤32°F`).

Human Impact: Primary driver of late-summer and fall hay fever and sinus pressure.
🐾 Dog Care Tip: Avoid overgrown trail edges in late summer and wipe your dog's muzzle and paws after walks if they are allergy-prone.
MOLD SPORES Humid / Post-Rain / Fall

🍄 Mold & Damp Spores (Alternaria, Cladosporium)

Unlike plant pollens that wash out during rain, fungal spores thrive in high humidity (`>65%`), damp soil, shaded mulch, and decaying autumn leaves.

Human Impact: Can trigger sinus congestion and respiratory irritation after rainstorms or while raking wet leaves.
🐾 Dog Care Tip: Dry between your dog's toes after wet walks and avoid letting them dig in damp leaf piles.
AIR QUALITY Scale: 0 to 500

📊 US EPA Air Quality Index (AQI)

Standardized EPA index combining particulate matter and ground-level ozone into a 0–500 scale for planning outdoor activity.

0–50 (Good): Ideal outdoors | 51–100 (Moderate): Generally acceptable | 101–150: Sensitive groups & flat-faced dogs should limit heavy exertion | 151+: Unhealthy for all.
PARTICULATE Size: < 2.5 µm

🌫️ PM2.5 Fine Inhalable Particulates

Fine inhalable particles from wildfire smoke, vehicle exhaust, and combustion. Small enough to reach deep into the lungs during heavy breathing or exercise.

Guidance: Ideal `< 12 µg/m³`. When `> 35 µg/m³`, reduce outdoor running/cardio and keep dog walks shorter and calmer.
PARTICULATE Size: 2.5 – 10 µm

💨 PM10 Coarse Dust & Particulates

Coarser airborne particles including windblown soil, road dust, and broken pollen fragments. Often elevated on dry, gusty days near busy roads.

Guidance: Can irritate eyes, throat, and nasal passages. Prefer grassy park paths over dusty roadside sidewalks when windy.
GAS / SMOG Peak: Sunny Afternoons

☀️ Ground-Level Ozone (O₃)

A reactive gas formed when strong sunlight reacts with vehicle and industrial emissions on warm days. Typically peaks between 2:00 PM and 7:00 PM.

Guidance: Can cause throat irritation and coughing during strenuous exercise. Schedule runs and longer dog walks for the morning hours.
