Allergen & Particulate Definitions: Human Health & Canine Adjunct Guide

BIO-ALLERGEN 01 PEAK: LATE SPRING – SUMMER

🌱 Grass / Turf Pollen (Poaceae)

Microscopic pollen shed by turf and pasture grasses (Bermuda, Timothy, Kentucky Bluegrass, Rye). Releases heavily on warm, dry, breezy mornings and spikes sharply during lawn mowing.

Human Impact: Classic summer hay fever (sneezing, runny nose, itchy/watery eyes) and grass-induced asthma.
🐾 Canine Adjunct: Absorbed directly through paw pads and belly skin; wipe paws with a damp cloth if your dog licks its toes.
BIO-ALLERGEN 02 PEAK: FEB – MAY

🌳 Arboreal / Tree Pollen (Oak, Birch, Alder, Pine)

Lightweight, wind-pollinated grains released in massive spring plumes by deciduous and coniferous trees. Can travel dozens of miles on brisk spring winds and coat outdoor surfaces in yellow dust.

Human Impact: Early-spring sinus congestion, post-nasal drip, allergic conjunctivitis, and oral allergy syndrome.
🐾 Canine Adjunct: Settles in dense fur and eyelashes; quick brushing after walks prevents indoor transfer.
BIO-ALLERGEN 03 PEAK: AUG – FIRST FROST

🌾 Ragweed & Herbaceous Weeds (Ambrosia, Mugwort)

A single ragweed plant can release up to 1 billion spiky pollen grains per season. Remains the #1 cause of late-summer and autumn seasonal allergies across North America until a hard freeze (`≤32°F`).

Human Impact: Intense autumn hay fever, sinus pressure, scratchy throat, and disrupted sleep.
🐾 Canine Adjunct: Spiky grains cling to muzzle and paws, triggering skin itching and occasional reverse sneezing.
BIO-ALLERGEN 04 PEAK: HUMID / POST-RAIN / AUTUMN

🍄 Fungal Mold & Damp Spores (Alternaria, Cladosporium)

Unlike plant pollens that wash out in rain, fungal spores multiply rapidly in high humidity (>65% RH), damp soil, shaded mulch, and decomposing autumn leaf piles.

Human Impact: Persistent sinus congestion, wheezing, and cough after rainstorms or while raking leaves.
🐾 Canine Adjunct: Dry between toes after wet walks to prevent dampness-related paw irritation.
ATMOS METRIC 01 SCALE: 0 TO 500

📊 US EPA Air Quality Index (AQI)

Official federal index combining particulate matter, ozone, carbon monoxide, and sulfur dioxide into a single 0–500 health benchmark for outdoor activity planning.

0–50 (Good): Ideal outdoors | 51–100 (Moderate): Acceptable | 101–150: Sensitive groups & flat-faced dogs limit exertion | 151+: Unhealthy for all.
ATMOS METRIC 02 SIZE: < 2.5 MICRONS (µm)

🌫️ PM2.5 Fine Inhalable Particulates

Microscopic combustion aerosols from wildfire smoke, vehicle exhaust, and industrial emissions. At less than 1/30th the width of a human hair, PM2.5 enters deep lung alveoli and the bloodstream.

Human & Pet Guidance: Ideal < 12 µg/m³. When > 35 µg/m³, avoid outdoor running/cardio, keep windows closed, and shorten dog walks.
ATMOS METRIC 03 SIZE: 2.5 – 10 MICRONS (µm)

💨 PM10 Coarse Dust & Bio-Particulates

Coarser airborne particles including windblown soil, road grit, construction dust, and broken pollen shells. Spikes during dry, gusty weather and near busy roadways.

Human & Pet Guidance: Irritates eyes, contact lenses, throat, and nasal passages. Heavier dust settles near ground level (6–24 inches high).
ATMOS METRIC 04 PEAK: HOT SUNNY AFTERNOONS

☀️ Tropospheric Ground-Level Ozone (O₃)

A reactive photochemical gas formed when strong UV sunlight cooks nitrogen oxides and VOC emissions on warm afternoons. Peaks between 2:00 PM and 7:00 PM.

Human & Pet Guidance: Causes chest tightness, dry cough, and airway inflammation during exercise. Schedule runs and walks in the morning.
