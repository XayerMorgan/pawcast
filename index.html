GRASS / TURF POLLEN GRASS / TURF POLLEN (Poaceae) Released by lawn grasses (Bermuda, Kentucky Bluegrass, Timothy, Rye). Unlike humans who inhale it, dogs absorb grass allergens directly through bare skin on paw pads, interdigital webbing, and the underbelly while walking. LOW (1.8 / 10)
ARBOREAL / TREE POLLEN ARBOREAL / TREE POLLEN (Oak, Birch, Alder, Pine) Fine, wind-dispersed spring pollen capable of traveling miles. Easily traps inside dense canine topcoats and eyelashes, triggering watery eyes (conjunctivitis), ear canal inflammation (otitis), and facial rubbing. LOW (1.2 / 10)
RAGWEED & HERBACEOUS WEEDS RAGWEED & HERBACEOUS WEEDS (Ambrosia, Mugwort, Nettle) Late-summer to autumn weeds producing spiky, microscopic pollen grains that cling stubbornly to fur around the muzzle and paws. A primary trigger for intense late-season canine skin itching (pruritus) and reverse sneezing. MODERATE (4.8 / 10)
FUNGAL MOLD & DAMP SPORES FUNGAL MOLD & DAMP SPORES (Alternaria, Cladosporium) Microscopic fungal spores thriving in damp soil, wet grass, shaded mulch, and decaying autumn leaves. Spikes during high humidity and after rain, causing respiratory irritation and secondary yeast infections between damp toes. LOW (2.4 / 10)

Allergen & Particulate Definitions: How Environmental Triggers Affect Dogs

TIP: DOGS ABSORB ~70% OF ALLERGENS THROUGH PAW & BELLY SKIN
BIO-ALLERGEN 01 PEAK: LATE SPRING – SUMMER

🌱 Grass / Turf Pollen (Poaceae)

Microscopic reproductive grains shed by cultivated turf and wild pasture grasses (Bermuda, Timothy, Kentucky Bluegrass, Orchard, and Rye). Because dogs walk directly through grass blades, exposure is primarily percutaneous (skin-contact) rather than just inhaled.

Canine Target Zones: Interdigital paw webbing, carpal pads, groin, axillae (armpits), and muzzle.
Mitigation Protocol: Rinse or wipe paws with cool water immediately after walks; keep lawn grass mowed short before seed heads form.
BIO-ALLERGEN 02 PEAK: FEB – MAY

🌳 Arboreal / Tree Pollen (Oak, Birch, Alder, Pine)

Lightweight, wind-pollinated grains released in massive plumes by deciduous and coniferous trees. Tree pollen stays suspended in dry, breezy air for days and settles as a fine dust across sidewalks, patios, and your dog's dorsal coat.

Canine Target Zones: Periocular skin (around eyes), pinnae (ear canals), and full body coat.
Mitigation Protocol: Brush coat outdoors after walks; use sterile saline eye wipes if tear staining or squinting occurs.
BIO-ALLERGEN 03 PEAK: AUG – FIRST FROST

🌾 Ragweed & Herbaceous Weeds (Ambrosia, Mugwort)

A single ragweed plant can release up to 1 billion spiky pollen grains per season. Under a microscope, weed pollen features barbed spines (echinate surface) that mechanically hook onto canine hair shafts and irritate mucous membranes.

Canine Target Zones: Nasal passages (triggering paroxysmal "reverse sneezing"), lips, chin, and belly skin.
Mitigation Protocol: Avoid overgrown trail edges and ditches in late summer/autumn; wipe muzzle and paws with a damp microfiber cloth.
BIO-ALLERGEN 04 PEAK: HUMID / POST-RAIN / AUTUMN

🍄 Fungal Mold & Damp Spores (Alternaria, Cladosporium)

Unlike plant pollens that wash away in rain, fungal spores multiply rapidly in high humidity (>65% RH), damp soil, shaded bark mulch, and decomposing autumn leaf piles. Remains active year-round in non-freezing climates.

Canine Target Zones: Bronchial airways and damp interdigital spaces (predisposing paws to Malassezia yeast overgrowth).
Mitigation Protocol: Thoroughly dry between toes after wet walks; prevent dogs from rooting in decaying leaf litter or wet mulch.
ATMOS METRIC 01 SCALE: 0 TO 500

📊 US EPA Air Quality Index (AQI)

Standardized federal benchmark measuring combined atmospheric pollutants. Because dogs exercise with high minute-ventilation (panting) and breathe through their mouths during exertion, they inhale 2–3x more air per pound of body weight than humans.

Canine Thresholds: 0–50: Nominal | 51–100: Monitor brachycephalic/senior dogs | 101–150: Shorten walks, no running | 151+: Indoor potty breaks only.
ATMOS METRIC 02 SIZE: < 2.5 MICRONS (µm)

🌫️ PM2.5 Fine Inhalable Particulates

Microscopic combustion aerosols from wildfire smoke, diesel exhaust, and power plants. At less than 1/30th the diameter of a human hair, PM2.5 bypasses the moist filtering turbinates of a dog's nose and embeds directly in deep lung alveoli.

Canine Risk Profile: High hazard for flat-faced breeds (Pugs, Bulldogs), dogs with collapsing trachea, bronchitis, or cardiac disease.
Safe Limit: Ideal < 12 µg/m³; restrict vigorous fetch or running when > 35 µg/m³.
ATMOS METRIC 03 SIZE: 2.5 – 10 MICRONS (µm)

💨 PM10 Coarse Dust & Bio-Particulates

Coarser airborne particles including road grit, windblown soil, brake dust, and fractured pollen shells. Because heavier particles settle near the pavement boundary layer (6 to 24 inches off the ground), dogs walk directly inside the highest concentration zone.

Canine Target Zones: Corneas (dry eye / keratitis), nasal mucosa (sneezing/discharge), and pharynx.
Mitigation Protocol: Prefer grassy park interiors over dusty roadside sidewalks on windy days.
ATMOS METRIC 04 PEAK: HOT SUNNY AFTERNOONS

☀️ Tropospheric Ground-Level Ozone (O₃)

A corrosive photochemical gas created when UV sunlight reacts with nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds from traffic. Ground-level ozone is heaviest right above sun-baked asphalt between 2:00 PM and 7:00 PM.

Canine Target Zones: Trachea and bronchial lining — causes oxidative airway irritation, dry coughing, and reduced stamina.
Mitigation Protocol: Schedule walks in early morning when ozone and pavement temperatures are both at daily minimums.
